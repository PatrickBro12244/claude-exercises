# Cymbal Knowledge Base

## 1. Jack DeJohnette — General Cymbal Approach

Jack DeJohnette is known for a highly expressive cymbal approach that emphasizes touch, dynamic control, articulation, and tonal color. His cymbal choices have varied throughout his career and have included Paiste cymbals.

DeJohnette's playing often demonstrates that cymbal sound is strongly influenced by stick technique, dynamics, placement, and musical context rather than cymbal specifications alone.

His cymbal sound should not be treated as a single fixed setup because his equipment has changed across different periods and recording situations.

---

## 2. Jack DeJohnette — Paiste Sound Creation Dark

Jack DeJohnette has been associated with Paiste's darker and more complex cymbal sounds.

Paiste Sound Creation Dark cymbals are known for darker tonal characteristics compared with brighter, more cutting cymbal designs. They can produce complex overtones and a softer, more controlled response.

The Sound Creation line is distinct from Paiste Signature and Paiste 2002 cymbals. A cymbal identified as Sound Creation Dark should not automatically be categorized as a Signature cymbal.

---

## 3. Jack DeJohnette — Paiste Signature

Jack DeJohnette has also been associated with Paiste Signature cymbals.

The Paiste Signature line is generally characterized by a clear, refined, and controlled sound. Signature cymbals are made from Paiste's proprietary Signature Bronze alloy.

Signature cymbals can provide strong projection while maintaining a relatively polished tonal character. This differs from the darker and more complex character typically associated with Sound Creation Dark models.

---

## 4. Jack DeJohnette — Paiste Signature Encore

Paiste Signature Encore cymbals are part of the Signature family and were designed with a darker, fuller, and more complex character than the standard Signature sound.

The Signature Encore concept is relevant when comparing DeJohnette-associated Paiste sounds because it occupies a different tonal position from a bright, highly cutting Signature cymbal.

A Signature Encore should therefore not be treated as interchangeable with a standard Paiste Signature cymbal.

---

## 5. Brian Blade — Cymbal Philosophy

Brian Blade's cymbal sound is strongly associated with dark, complex, musical cymbals that respond well to subtle playing.

Blade's approach frequently uses cymbals as part of the musical texture rather than simply as loud timekeeping instruments.

His sound emphasizes wash, decay, interaction between cymbals, and dynamic response. The cymbals can produce a broad sound at low and medium dynamics while still providing enough definition for jazz and modern acoustic music.

## 6. Brian Blade — 24-Inch A Ride

Brian Blade has used a 24-inch A ride cymbal.

The 24-inch size contributes to a large playing surface, broad wash, and substantial low-frequency presence. A large ride can produce a wide and sustained sound without necessarily requiring extremely heavy construction.

The 24-inch A ride is useful as a reference point when comparing Blade's larger cymbal sounds against smaller jazz rides.

## 23. Cymbal Weight Does Not Fully Determine Sound

Cymbal weight is useful for comparing cymbals, but it does not completely determine their sound.

Two cymbals with the same diameter and similar weight can sound substantially different because of differences in alloy, hammering, lathing, profile, bow shape, bell design, thickness distribution, and manufacturing process.

For example, Kyle's 22-inch cymbals have different weights:

* Pandora: 2450 g
* Avedis Reissue: 2614 g
* K High Definition: 2800 g

The weight difference provides useful physical information, but it should not be treated as a complete description of their tonal characteristics.

---

## 24. Cymbal Size and Wash

Larger cymbals generally provide a larger playing surface and can produce a broad, sustained wash.

A 24-inch ride can have a different physical response from a 20-inch cymbal even when the smaller cymbal is relatively thin.

However, diameter alone does not determine sustain or darkness. Weight distribution and construction are also important.

---

## 25. Jazz Cymbal Characteristics

Jazz-oriented cymbals are often selected for musical complexity, dynamic response, wash, stick articulation, and tonal interaction.

A cymbal does not need to be extremely dark or extremely thin to work in jazz.

The drummer's touch is an important part of the resulting sound. The same cymbal can produce significantly different results depending on where and how strongly it is played.
